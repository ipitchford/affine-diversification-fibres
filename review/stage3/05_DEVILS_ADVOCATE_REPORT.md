## Reviewer identity

Identifiability and meta-science specialist instructed to construct the strongest case against the paper's core contribution and to test its logic without evaluating journal fit or duplicating detailed proof review.

## Strongest counter-argument

The manuscript does something mathematically respectable: conditional on an exact pulled signal and a chosen restriction class, it computes worst-case compatible histories rather than mistaking a finite trajectory cloud for the whole congruence class. The evidence record is also candid about the failed H4 gate.

The strongest opposing case is that the paper perfects the least decision-relevant layer of the problem. Real analysts do not know the pulled trajectory `F`; they estimate it from a finite, uncertain, model-dependent timetree. They also do not know a biologically privileged global turnover cap or a deterministic mapping from fossils to latent species richness. Exact endpoints conditional on uncertain inputs and contestable restrictions can therefore be precisely wrong in the sense most consequential for scientific interpretation. The paper's own benchmark supports this concern. H2 shows that a finite cloud is not a proof of a mathematical endpoint—something already expected for finite stochastic exploration—while H4 shows that exact certification fails the manuscript's own threshold for changing preset decisions. The 532 changes are generated by artificial query thresholds, not demonstrated biological decisions, and abrupt-signal cases vanish from H4 because the frozen comparator cannot return under its rate ceiling. A more parsimonious research strategy is to spend effort on identifiable restricted models, signal uncertainty, fossil observation processes or hypothesis-driven biological constraints, using the affine geometry only as an internal sensitivity tool. On the present evidence, the certificate is a rigorous audit instrument, not yet a field-changing complement.

## Ignored alternative explanations or paths

1. **Finite-cloud overconfidence is a reporting failure, not necessarily a sampler failure.** A cheaper solution may be to prohibit endpoint language for finite samples and require convergence/sensitivity summaries without adding a new general-purpose certificate.
2. **Hypothesis-driven restricted models may dominate worst-case bounds.** Biologically motivated finite-dimensional or regularized models can answer target questions with interpretable uncertainty, even though they do not identify the unrestricted history.
3. **Additional observations can change the identification problem.** Fossil sampling, hidden birth information or other data may restore identification more directly than optimizing an extant-only congruence class.
4. **Probabilistic rather than worst-case summaries may be the practical target.** If users care about posterior or regularized plausibility, mathematically feasible boundary histories may receive negligible weight and exact worst-case intervals may be too conservative.

## Unexamined premise

The paper assumes that sharpness over the largest stated conditional class is the appropriate decision criterion. That premise is not universal. In some scientific settings, robustness over a defensible neighbourhood, posterior decision risk or falsification of a small hypothesis family is more relevant than the global extrema of every mathematically feasible path. The manuscript should explain when worst-case sharpness is the right objective and when it is deliberately conservative.

## Observations (non-defects)

- Retaining the failed H4 decision significantly strengthens trust; it is not evidence against the integrity of the work.
- The conditional theorem package may remain publishable and useful even if the broad adoption narrative is reduced.
- The paper already contains many of the correct limitations; the remaining problem is that those limitations are sometimes more important than the corresponding positive result.
